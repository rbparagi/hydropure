The science, simply

You don't have to take our word for it

Kangen Water stands apart in three ways you can actually see and measure for yourself — its dissolved hydrogen, its alkalinity, and the size of its molecular clusters.

Property 01 · Molecular hydrogen

Charged with hydrogen, the smallest molecule there is

As water flows over the machine's titanium plates, electrolysis fills it with dissolved molecular hydrogen (H₂). That hydrogen is what gives Kangen Water its negative ORP, or antioxidant potential, which you can measure in millivolts.

Why it's made at your tap: hydrogen escapes from water over time, so bottled “hydrogen water” fades on the shelf. Kangen Water is made fresh as you pour it, at around −400 mV or lower. Soda and tap water sit at +200 to +400 mV.
Property 02 · Alkalization

Four cups. One drop each. Only one turns purple.

We added one drop of pH reagent to four everyday drinks. The soft drink turns pink, which means acidic. Premium bottled water and RO water turn yellow, which means slightly acidic. Only Kangen Water turns purple, which means alkaline.

Property 03 · Micro-clustering

Broken into smaller clusters

Enagic describes ionization as breaking water's molecular clusters into smaller groupings — a concept known as “micro-clustering,” and the idea behind the well-known tea-bag test.

The tea-bag test: a bag dropped in cold Kangen Water appears to brew faster than in cold regular water — a simple test often used to illustrate the idea.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked

Kangen Water® is ionized alkaline water produced by Enagic's water ionizers through electrolysis. "Kangen" is Japanese for "return to origin" — water restored to a clean, mineral-rich, antioxidant state. It is alkaline (pH 8.5–9.5), hydrogen-rich, and has a negative ORP.

RO water is acidic (pH 6–7) and strips out all minerals along with contaminants. Kangen Water retains beneficial minerals like calcium and magnesium, is alkaline, and is rich in dissolved molecular hydrogen, which gives it the antioxidant potential (negative ORP) that RO water simply cannot provide.

Hydrogen is the smallest molecule there is, so it escapes easily: from plastic bottles while they sit on the shelf, and from any glass soon after it's poured. A Kangen machine makes it fresh at your tap, right when you pour, with no bottles to keep buying.
